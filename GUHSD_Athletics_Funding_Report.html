Executive Summary

Five findings

  1. Per-student spending: GUHSD spends $21,644 per ADA, above Sweetwater, San Dieguito, Oceanside, San Marcos, and Poway. San Dieguito spends 28% less per ADA and employs full-time classified athletic trainers.
  2. Athletic trainer staffing: GUHSD pays its AT as a $22,500 certificated stipend. Six of seven verified SD County peers — including districts spending 28% less per ADA — employ the AT as a full-time classified healthcare role with benefits and BOC/NATA certification required.
  3. Coaching stipend structure: Helix Charter (in the Grossmont League) pays head varsity football $1,190–$1,640 more than GUHSD at every experience tier (2025–26 schedule). Sweetwater pays 40% more at entry level. GUHSD caps stipends at 52 positions per school for 24+ sports; additional coaches are unpaid or paid from ASB/booster funds.
  4. Athletic Director compensation: GUHSD's AD tops out at $6,227 — below SDUHSD ($7,354), SDUSD ($12,636 with 208-day work year), Helix Charter ($10,458–$13,675 + Asst AD line), Steele Canyon Charter ($13,434 — corrected from primary-source 2025-26 schedule), and SMUSD's 15% salary differential structure (~$10,000+). Only Sweetwater's flat $4,256 AD stipend sits below GUHSD's top tier.
  5. Officials funding: GUHSD allocates $22,000 per school. At Granite Hills (verified FY 2025-26), actual competition overhead — Arbiter-paid officials plus tournament entry fees — totaled $57,393. The district allocation covered ~38% of that at one site alone; the remaining $35,393 was absorbed by sport-specific trust accounts funded through ASB and booster fundraising. Scaled across 9 comprehensive sites, the district-wide ASB-paid cost is estimated at $162K–$297K per year.
  6. Coaching paid through ASB: Granite Hills paid $96,009 from student trust accounts to compensate 53 coaches not on district stipend, authorized through the GUHSD "Request To Pay Coach – Trust" Google Form and routed through district HR via PAF. GUHSD allocates 52 stipends ($313,200) to Granite Hills, split across 58 coach positions (e.g., one frosh football stipend split four ways at 0.5 each). An additional $27,710 in non-league officials, coach clearance, LiveScan, and league patches was invoiced to ASB, for a FY 2025-26 total of $123,719+. With the $35,393 officials difference, the ASB/booster total at one site is ~$159,000+; scaled district-wide, an estimated $1.0M–$1.4M+. See one-pager #6.

The conversion charters

Two of GUHSD's own conversion charters, Helix Charter HS (La Mesa) and Steele Canyon Charter HS (Spring Valley), operate inside the Grossmont League. Both charters' athletic compensation structures were verified for this report:

  • Helix Charter pays head varsity football $9,251 at top tier (2025–26) — $1,640 more than GUHSD ($7,611). Helix's underlying teacher base salary is 16.3% higher than GUHSD's (Class 3 Step 1: $80,444 vs. $69,187).
  • Steele Canyon Charter uses GUHSD's identical percentages (7%/8%/10%/11%) but applies them to Class 3 Step 4 ($79,021.25), not Step 1, which puts head varsity football top tier at $8,692 (+$1,082 vs. GUHSD) and AD at $13,434 (+116% over GUHSD's $6,227). Steele Canyon also credits GUHSD experience as equivalent on hire and requires a CA teaching credential. AD release time at Steele Canyon is 1 period/year per the verified 2025-26 schedule.

Enrollment and funding

California's Local Control Funding Formula (LCFF) allocates district revenue by average daily attendance (ADA), so each student who enrolls elsewhere reduces district funding. Both conversion charters in the Grossmont League pay more per coach and per Athletic Director than GUHSD (see Pillars 3 and 3b).

The reserves context

GUHSD announced 61 layoff notices in February 2025 (49 credentialed, 12 classified, plus closure of the child care center) citing a $2.5 million deficit driven in part by declining enrollment. At the same time, per public reporting, the district maintains $31.2 million in general fund reserves — $20.8 million above the 3% state-required minimum (FY2026-27 adopted budget). For context, the estimated district-wide ASB-paid cost for officials (~$162,000–$297,000 per year, scaled from verified Granite Hills data) represents well under 1% of the district's reserves.

The share-of-budget comparison

Helix's total operating expenses in FY 2024 were approximately $41.4 million (per its Form 990). Its $693K athletic stipend spend is ~1.67% of that total. GUHSD's $2.82M district-wide coaching stipend spend (verified Sports Grid 2025-26) is ~0.88% of its $321M general fund, so Helix's share is roughly 1.9× GUHSD's.

Helix's broader stipend schedule also has roughly twice as many non-athletic named line items as GUHSD — across academics (adds Journalism Sponsor, Speech & Debate Coach, S&D Assistant Coach), arts (adds Band Assistant Director, Flags Advisor, Musical Theater Music Director, Choreographer), and leadership (20+ lines including Senior Project Coordinator at $9,000 flat, WASC Chair, ELD Coord, CTE Grant Coord, Work Experience Coord, etc.). The "GUHSD experience transfers" clause — verbatim from HTA Contract §13.1.4.1

"Coaches shall be credited with a maximum of 7 years outside coaching experience in other districts or the equivalent in the verified sport to be coached. All experience within the Grossmont Union High School district in the sport to be coached shall be credited to the members."

Practical effect: a 10+-year GUHSD head varsity coach moving to Helix lands at the top of Helix's schedule immediately on hire — experience carries over. The stipend difference between GUHSD top-tier ($7,611) and Helix top-tier (~$9,251) becomes a 21.5% raise in stipend compensation alone for making the move, before accounting for playoffs, larger leadership stipend pool, and the additional compensation items on asterisked roles.

Coach mobility to Helix

Helix enrolls 2,571 students (2025–26 CDE). Because Helix credits GUHSD coaching experience on hire (HTA Contract §13.1.4.1), a GUHSD head coach at the top experience step who moves to Helix would earn roughly $1,600–$2,500 more per year in stipend.

GUHSD pays its certificated Athletic Trainers $35/hour — roughly half of what the California professional association recommends as a minimum for any region of California, and well below half the appropriate rate for Southern California (Regions 5–6 at $67–$73/hour). CATA also notes its recommended rate "does NOT include any expenses related to health care supplies (e.g. athletic tape, crutches, or emergency equipment)" — those costs sit on top of the per-diem rate.

Three independent benchmarks bear on GUHSD's AT model:

  1. Pierpoint 2018 (student safety): 6× / 3× higher recurrent injury rates in girls' soccer / girls' basketball at schools without ATs
  2. Morgan Hill OCR 2023 (Title IX): Title IX requires equitable AT access regardless of booster funding source
  3. CATA 2022 (market rate): GUHSD's $35/hr is roughly half the state-association-recommended minimum for Southern California

Source: California Athletic Trainers Association, Recommended Minimum Per Diem Pay Rate: On-Site Athletic Training Medical Care (provided by GUHSD athletics administration).

Regulatory context

California is the only U.S. state without athletic-trainer licensure. AB 796 (Chapter 899, Statutes of 2024) — signed by Governor Newsom on September 29, 2024 — established title protection only, not licensure. It makes it unlawful to hold oneself out as "athletic trainer," "certified athletic trainer," or similar without CAATE-program graduation and BOC certification, but does not require schools to employ ATs.

Title IX: the Morgan Hill resolution (October 2023)

In October 2023, the U.S. Department of Education's Office for Civil Rights (OCR) resolved a Title IX investigation into Morgan Hill Unified School District (Live Oak HS) involving booster-funded athletics.

OCR's findings at Live Oak HS:

  • Girls' programs "may not have had similar quality equipment, equal access to the school's weight room and athletic trainer, equal access to the school's athletic locker room, or equitable publicity"
  • Booster funding described by a Live Oak coach as "the wild west" — teams "fend for themselves" for funding
  • OCR found disparate benefits (equipment, facilities, AT access) between boys' and girls' programs

Resolution agreement — district commitments:

  • "Create committees to ensure that differences in fundraising between teams will not create disparities in athletic equipment, uniforms or supplies based on sex"
  • "Ensure the school's girls' and boys' athletics teams have equitable access to the school's athletic trainer, weight room, and training facilities"
"If booster clubs provide benefits that create disparities based on gender, the school is responsible for offsetting those disparate benefits. The origins of the money that created the inequities does not serve as a defense for the district and does not mitigate the obligation to remedy those inequities." — OCR / NFHS Title IX guidance

California's two-tier coaching workforce

Per CA AB 346 (2010), non-certificated individuals in paid or volunteer coaching positions must hold an Activity Supervisor Clearance Certificate (ASCC) issued by the Commission on Teacher Credentialing. The ASCC is a fingerprint/background-check clearance — it does NOT authorize instruction or grant teaching-credential-equivalent status. This codifies California's two-tier coaching workforce: certificated teacher-coaches with classroom access and credential-level training, and ASCC-cleared walk-on coaches with a background check only.

LCFF value of attendance

In California, LCFF funding is based on ADA (average daily attendance). Each percentage-point increase in attendance across GUHSD's ~14,835 ADA base is roughly 148 additional ADA (1% × 14,835). At the CA HSD LCFF base grant rate for grades 9-12 (~$11,500/ADA), that is ~$1.7M per year; including supplemental/concentration grants, ~$1.9M+ per year.
