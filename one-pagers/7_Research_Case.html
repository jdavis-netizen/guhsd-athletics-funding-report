Research on Athletics Participation
GUHSD Athletics Data Brief Granite Hills Athletics
May 2026
Published research compiled by the National Federation of State High School Associations (NFHS) reports that athletes have higher attendance, graduation rates, and GPAs than non-athletes, with larger differences for low-income students. AEI's April 2026 report (n=262,600) is the most recent study.
Graduation
99.4% vs 93.5%
Athlete vs. non-athlete graduation rate. Athletes ~17× less likely to drop out. Overton, North Carolina, 2001.
Attendance
−12.9 pp
Lower chronic absenteeism for athletes vs. non-athletes. Effect ~2× larger for low-income students. AEI 2026 (n=262,600).
Academic Performance
+0.81 GPA
Athlete GPA 2.98 vs. non-athlete 2.17 — nearly a full letter grade. Overton 2001 NC. Coe 2006: athletes ~10% better in core academics.

Six categories of evidence · 25 years of cited research

OutcomeStudy (n, location, year)Finding
Attendance Malkus & Hollon · AEI · 2026 (n=262,600) Athletes are 12.9 pp less likely to be chronically absent. Effect ~2× larger for FRPM-eligible students.
Attendance Overton · NC · 2001 Athletes miss 6.3 days/year vs. 11.9 days for non-athletes — nearly half as many absences.
Graduation Overton · NC · 2001 Athlete graduation rate 99.4% vs. non-athlete 93.5%. Athlete dropout rate 0.6% vs. 10.32%.
Graduation / GPA Lumpkin & Favor · KS · 2012 (n=~140,000) Athletes graduate at higher rates and earn higher GPAs. Students of color show much larger gains.
College Attainment Gardner · 2008 · Lipscomb · 2007 Activity participation positively associated with post-secondary attainment 2 and 8 years after HS; 5% increase in BA-attainment.
Discipline / Safety Overton · NC · 2001 Discipline referral rate 33.3% (athletes) vs. 41.8% (non-athletes).
School Safety Sage · 2010 · Veliz & Shakib · 2012 Increased activity funding ↔ decreased risk of physical assault on educators. Higher sports participation ↔ fewer serious crimes & suspensions.
Mental Health & Substance Use Taliaferro · 2008 · Harrison & Narayan · MN · 2003 (n=50,168) Sports participation significantly reduces risk of hopelessness and suicidal behaviors in both males and females. Significantly lower alcohol, marijuana, and vandalism involvement.
★ Findings by income. Everson & Millsap, 2005 (n = 480,000+ SAT scores): the impact of activity participation on academic outcomes is larger than the impact of family socioeconomic factors. Marsh & Kleitman, 2002: activities benefit socioeconomically disadvantaged students as much or more than advantaged students. Lumpkin & Favor, 2012: pay-to-play policies "discriminate against students who do not have the financial means."
